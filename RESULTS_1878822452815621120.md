# Gaia DR3 1878822452815621120 (SB9 2188): a known stellar binary scored as "likely substellar"

Status: exploratory, written 23 Nov 2026. Not pre-registered and not part of any confirmatory test. It uses only public DR3 and SB9/SBX data and none of the 76 sealed objects.

## Why we checked it

When we cross-matched with SB9 (RESULTS_EXTERNAL_RV.md), this star matched SB9 system 2188. That system is a single-lined binary with P = 3977 d, published by Latham et al. 2002 (AJ 124, 1144). Gaia DR3 gives it a different orbit: P = 1009 ± 138 d, e = 0.45, i = 84°, a0 = 0.55 mas. The first-order fitted mass is 16 MJ. GAIA-FP v3.2 gives P_impostor = 0.062, so the verdict is "likely_substellar".

Gaia's period is 0.97 of the DR3 baseline (1038 d). That puts the star in group N of H21.

We used all 79 CfA radial velocities from SBX (JD 2445190–2450065, 4875 d span, median error 0.55 km/s). They are saved in `realdata/external/sbx_2188_velocities.csv`.

## Results

1. The CfA velocities need only one orbit. A single Keplerian fit gives P = 4042 d, e = 0.34, K = 2.66 km/s and residual rms 0.45 km/s. The fit has χ² = 49 for 73 degrees of freedom, so there is no extra scatter to explain.

2. There is no significant 1009 d signal in the velocities.
   - The residual periodogram (300–3000 d) peaks at 373 d, with false-alarm probability 0.07.
   - We added a second Keplerian with Gaia's P (±1σ), e and ω fixed. The best fit has P2 = 1124 d and K2 = 0.38 km/s, but it only improves χ² by Δχ² = 5.0. Our 99% null threshold is 7.9, so this is not a detection.
   - We also injected signals and tried to recover them, using 30 random phases per K. The detection rates were 30% at K = 0.2, 53% at 0.3, 77% at 0.41 and 87% at 0.5 km/s.

   These old velocities therefore cannot rule out Gaia's K = 0.41 km/s at 95% confidence. They do not support it either.

3. The known stellar orbit can mimic a ~1000 d astrometric orbit.
   - The SB9 mass function is f(m) = 0.0067 M☉. That means the companion is an M dwarf: 0.19 M☉ if i = 84°, 0.44 M☉ if i = 30°.
   - At 62 pc, the photocentre orbit from that companion is 15–30 mas.
   - Inside the 1038 d DR3 window, we removed the best linear plus quadratic trend (what proper motion and acceleration can absorb). The part that remains has a median of 0.37 mas at i = 84°, 0.50 mas at 60° and 0.97 mas at 30°. In 24–68% of orbital phases it is at least 0.55 mas.
   - Gaia's fitted a0 is 0.55 mas, so the numbers match. The DR3 orbital model has no acceleration terms, which makes leakage into the fitted orbit even easier.

## Interpretation

The simplest explanation: Gaia saw about 3 years of a known 11-year stellar orbit and fitted that piece as a 1009 d orbit. The 16 MJ "companion" is then probably an artefact, and the true companion is an M dwarf of about 0.2–0.4 M☉. The RV data alone can't prove this. Both checks above (no velocity signal; about the right amount of leftover curvature) point the same way.

## What this means for GAIA-FP

- The frozen pipeline confidently called this star substellar (P_impostor 0.06). It is a real example of the failure mode that H21 (baseline trap) was written to test before DR4: a Gaia period near the baseline and a fitted mass that is too low.
- One example proves nothing statistically, and H21 stays as registered.
- The test will come at DR4. With about 66 months of data, the 3977 d orbit is more than half covered.
  - If DR4 gives P ≈ 4000 d or a mass far above 80 MJ, our explanation is confirmed.
  - If DR4 again gives ~1000 d with a similar a0, our explanation is wrong.

  We are recording this prediction here before DR4. It is not an OSF registration.

- Telescope time: we should not spend Shamakhy time on this star before DR4. A single extra spectrum cannot separate the two cases.

## Limits

- The CfA spectra are from 1982–1995. Gaia DR3 covers 2014–2017.
- The phase of the long orbit in the Gaia window is uncertain: our fit gives 4042 d versus the catalogue's 3977 d.
- We treated the M dwarf as dark (β ≈ 0). A small flux contribution would lower a0 slightly.
- The injection test used 30 phases per K, so the detection rates are uncertain by about ±8%.
